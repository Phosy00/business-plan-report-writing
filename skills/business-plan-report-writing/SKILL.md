# Business Plan Report: Writing and Finalising

Use this skill when helping a student turn research into a business or digital transformation plan report, review drafts, and prepare the final file for submission (e.g. via Turnitin). The student writes the body; Claude checks, explains and fixes only what the student agrees to.

## 1. Working rules

- Reply in the student's language. If the student writes in another language, give any English text (sentences, emails) with a translation alongside.
- Restate each new requirement and wait for confirmation before editing a file. Discuss first, then edit.
- In bilingual drafts, never change the student's own-language text. Edit the English only, and highlight every change.
- When the student rewrites a sentence in their own words, treat it as theirs. Flag a claim only if it misstates a source; say so once and let the student decide.
- Separate the student's inference (keep it, add may / could / appears) from factual claims (need a source). Before calling a sentence unsupported, check whether it is the student's own analysis.
- Do not rewrite text to lower an AI-detection score. Offer bullet points of what each paragraph must say so the student writes it, and remind them to keep drafts and declare AI use honestly.
- Check work against the harshest critic before replying, and correct your own earlier mistakes openly.
- If a list of problems is agreed, make the fixes in the same turn; do not send a checklist and stop.

## 2. Structure (follow the course template)

Use the template's section order and listed topics, with subheadings. A typical split for 2,500 words: Introduction 250, Situational Analysis 750, Problem Solving 600, Implementation 400, Ethical and Legal 400, Conclusion 100. Check the brief for the actual split.

- Introduction: what the company does, the scope (division and channel), why now (data with source), the objective in one sentence using the word "objective", and the digital solution in one line.
- Situational analysis: value proposition, vision, business model, internal analysis (e.g. people, data, technology), external analysis (PESTEL, Five Forces citing Porter (1979), 2 to 3 named competitors compared on the same criteria, changing customer expectations), SWOT, TOWS, VRIO, SMART objective. Raise the problem only after the analysis tools.
- Problem solving: the priority as a How Might We question with no solution inside it, what the plan does not pursue, a priority matrix, the digital solution (customer channel plus back-end), MVP, validation stages, capabilities, costs and risks.
- Implementation: governance, a timeline with owners in the main text (not only in the appendix), KPIs.
- Ethical and legal analysis, then a conclusion covering objective, priority, digital solution, delivery and checks, with no new ideas.
- The appendix supports the body (check the page limit); essential content stays in the body.

## 3. Framework definitions to get right

- Rogers (2023), The Digital Transformation Roadmap: a shared vision has four elements, namely future landscape, right to win, North Star impact and business theory. The North Star is a long-term impact statement (why), not a numeric target. The numeric target belongs to the SMART objective and plan KPI. Keep figures consistent with the North Star wording.
- TOWS (Ferrell and Hartline, Marketing Strategy): match strengths to opportunities (the source of the claimed competitive advantage), convert weaknesses, minimise where a weakness meets a threat. Keep TOWS (what the advantage is) separate from VRIO (whether it lasts).
- VRIO (Barney): valuable, rare, inimitable, organised. Valuable, rare and hard to copy but not yet organised means an unrealised competitive advantage. Do not claim inimitability without naming who could copy it.
- KPI chain: one plan KPI, one priority KPI, stage KPIs. State each figure once and refer back instead of repeating it.
- Competitor comparison: compare every competitor on the same criteria (e.g. customer-facing tools versus back-end use of data). Do not compare one firm's internal forecasting with another firm's customer tool.

## 4. Evidence checks

- Primary sources first (annual reports, results announcements, legislation, company newsrooms). Fetch the original and quote the exact wording when checking a figure.
- Do not overstate a source: "partly offset" is not "the main drag"; "up to half" is not "half".
- A framework book proves a method, not a company fact. A study of several anonymised companies does not prove what one named company does: hedge the claim.
- For a time series, report from the earliest relevant year to the latest, and say plainly when the latest year is not published.
- Say "not sure" when a page number, date or figure cannot be confirmed, and name what the student must check (e.g. paywalled market research).

## 5. Word count

- Count the body from the Introduction to before the References, including headings, tables, captions and citations (check what the brief includes). Report the parts separately.
- If the brief counts figures, text inside figure images counts too, but word processors do not count it. Count it separately and add it once only.
- Word processors count words by spaces: "p.63" is one word, "p. 63" is two. Check the effect before changing citation spacing.
- If the brief allows plus or minus 10%, say so. Do not cut the student's sentences without asking; propose the smallest cuts first.

## 6. Referencing (Harvard, Cite Them Right)

Check entries against Cite Them Right Online (usually available through the university library's database list).

- Books and reports: Author (Year) Title in italics. Edition. Publisher. No place of publication. A report with a URL uses "Available at:" instead of a publisher.
- Journal articles: 'Article title', Journal in italics, volume(issue), pp. or article number.
- Online news: Author (Year) 'Title', Source in italics, day month. Available at: URL (Accessed: date).
- Web pages: Organisation (Year) Title in italics. Available at: URL (Accessed: date).
- UK Acts: Title Year, c. number. Statutory instruments: Title Year (SI year/number), with the SI number not in italics.
- Up to three authors listed in full; four or more use et al. (unless the institution requires all names).
- Check italics run by run in the Word file: only the title or source name is italic, not dates, descriptors or SI numbers.
- Every in-text citation (body and appendix) must appear in the list, and every list entry must be cited somewhere.

## 7. Formatting and final PDF check

Before the student submits, open the exported PDF page by page and confirm:

- The required font (e.g. Arial or Calibri) is embedded, including italic; body text size, line spacing and alignment match the brief; tables and figures use the required smaller size; heading style is consistent.
- Page numbers start where the brief says; contents page numbers sit on the same line as their headings (remove duplicate tabs, update the field).
- Appendix page references in the body match the printed page numbers; the appendix is within the page limit; table headers are correct across page breaks.
- Figures match the text (e.g. North Star wording); no highlights or draft brackets remain.
- Anonymous marking: no name anywhere, including file properties; student number only where required.
- The AI declaration states truthfully what AI did (e.g. finding and checking sources, drawing figures, drafting help); any declaration checklist is placed where the brief says and its tick boxes still display after a font change.
- One file in the required format and name, submitted before the deadline.

A preview PDF made without the real fonts is only for checking layout; the student exports the final PDF on their own computer.
